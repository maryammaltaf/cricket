Why go

Cricket, then the Cape

October and November are South African spring: long sunny days, wildflowers, whales still off the coast and jacaranda season in Pretoria. Between matches you get a coastline that easily matches anything on the Vietnam list.

Good to know

Essentials

Visa

  • Required for Pakistani passports. Apply at the VFS / Gerry's centre in Karachi (Bahria Complex IV).
  • Fees: PKR 3,700 visa fee plus about USD 50 for the appointment, per person.
  • Bank statements: last 3 months, bank-stamped, no older than 7 days at submission. Each of you needs funds in your own name; spouse sponsorship isn't accepted, so transfer funds with a short covering letter if needed.
  • Processing: about 10 calendar days. Apply 6–8 weeks before you fly with hotel bookings, return tickets and employer NOCs.

Money

  • Currency: South African rand. About R16.4 per US dollar.
  • Cards work almost everywhere; keep a little cash for tips and markets.
  • Tipping 10–15% at restaurants; R10–20 for car guards and petrol attendants.

Weather

  • Cape Town: spring, roughly 13–25°C, sunny and often windy. Bring a light jacket for evenings at Newlands.
  • Johannesburg: warm days around 25°C, short afternoon thunderstorms.
  • Strong sun: sunscreen and hats for day games.

Halal food

  • Cape Town has a large Cape Malay Muslim community. Bo-Kaap and the city centre are full of halal restaurants.
  • In Johannesburg look to Fordsburg and Mayfair; many Sandton restaurants are certified too.
  • Look for SANHA or MJC halal certificates on the wall.

Getting around

  • Uber and Bolt everywhere; cheap and the safest default after dark.
  • Gautrain links Johannesburg airport, Sandton and Pretoria.
  • Driving is on the left, like Pakistan. A Pakistani licence plus an International Driving Permit covers the Garden Route road trip.

Practical

  • Time: South Africa is 3 hours behind Karachi.
  • Plugs: type M/N (big round three-pin). Bring a universal adapter.
  • SIM: Vodacom or MTN at the airport, passport needed.
  • Safety: normal big-city care. Don't walk around CBDs after dark; keep phones out of sight in traffic.
